**211: Connecting People with Essential Local Resources**
==================================================================

211 is a nationwide service that connects people with local resources and support for basic needs such as housing, food, transportation, and health care. Powered by thousands of local experts, the network offers confidential, anonymous assistance 24 hours a day, seven days a week. Readers can learn more about the organization’s work on its [official website](https://211.org/about-us).

## Organization Background
211 was designated by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in 2000 as the three‑digit number for information and referrals to social services and other assistance. The service is delivered by more than 200 local organizations across the United States, including United Ways, Goodwill, Community Action Partnerships, and local crisis centers. Each local agency employs a team of compassionate, highly trained community specialists who are available around the clock to help callers find appropriate resources.

## Communities Served
The 211 network serves individuals and families throughout the United States who are seeking help with basic needs. Most contacts involve requests for assistance with housing, food, transportation, and health care. In a typical year, the network responds to more than 13 million requests for help, and in 2025 it facilitated over 19 million connections to resources and services. The service is accessible to anyone, with calls kept confidential and able to be made anonymously.

## Programs and Services
211’s primary function is to provide information and referrals to local programs and services that address a range of needs, including:
- Housing and utility assistance  
- Food programs and benefits  
- Health care and mental health support  
- Substance use resources  
- Crisis and emergency support  
- Disaster recovery assistance  
- Caregiver resources  

These services are delivered locally by the more than 200 affiliated agencies; the national network coordinates standards and supports the local partners but does not directly operate service delivery in individual communities.
