**Questions to Ask Before Joining a Church**  
==================================================

Finding a church home is more than picking a place to worship; it’s discerning where God may be inviting you to grow, serve, and belong. This guide builds on the earlier parts of the series—helping you move from recognizing a healthy congregation to asking the right questions before committing.

## Scripture Foundation  

> **Scripture Reflection:**  
> “And I tell you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of Hades will not overcome it.” (Matthew 16:18)  
> Scripture quotations are from the New International Version (NIV).  

Jesus’ promise reminds us that the church is His body, built on truth and love. As we consider joining, we look for signs of that foundation in teaching, fellowship, and mission.  

Key passages that shape our questions:  

- **Acts 2:42‑47** – The early believers devoted themselves to the apostles’ teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, and prayer. A healthy church nurtures these four rhythms.  
- **Ephesians 4:1‑6** – Unity, humility, gentleness, patience, and bearing with one another in love are marks of a community worthy of our commitment.  
- **Hebrews 10:24‑25** – We are called to spur one another toward love and good deeds, not neglecting to meet together.  

These texts guide us to ask about doctrine, relationships, service, and regular gathering.

## What This Looks Like in Daily Life  

When you visit a congregation, observe how the Scripture‑shaped values appear in everyday practice:  

- **Teaching:** Does the preaching explain Scripture clearly, point to Christ, and encourage application? Are there opportunities for deeper study (small groups, classes)?  
- **Fellowship:** Do people greet newcomers warmly? Is there evidence of genuine care—meals, prayer chains, support in hardship?  
- **Worship:** Is the focus on honoring God rather than performance? Are prayers, songs, and communion (if observed) done with reverence and inclusivity?  
- **Service:** How does the church serve the local community and global mission? Are there clear ways to use your gifts?  
- **Leadership:** Are pastors and elders approachable, accountable, and transparent about vision and finances?  

Noticing these patterns helps you discern whether a community aligns with the biblical picture of the church.

## Questions for Reflection  

Use these questions as you visit, talk with members, and pray over your decision. They assume you’ve already looked for health (see part 2) and now move toward discernment.  

1. **Doctrine & Teaching:** What does the church believe about the essentials of the faith (e.g., the Trinity, salvation through Christ, the authority of Scripture)? How are these teachings communicated and lived out?  
2. **Community Life:** How does the church foster genuine relationships across ages, backgrounds, and life stages? Are there regular opportunities for fellowship beyond Sunday services?  
3. **Mission & Service:** In what tangible ways does the congregation love its neighbors locally and globally? How can I see myself participating?  
4. **Leadership & Accountability:** How are leaders selected, trained, and held accountable? Is there a clear process for addressing concerns or conflict?  
5. **Discipleship & Growth:** What pathways exist for spiritual growth (Bible studies, mentorship, retreats)? How does the church encourage personal holiness and obedience to Jesus?  
6. **Culture & Grace:** How does the church respond to doubt, struggle, or have been hurt by church before? Is there a posture and a culture of confession and forgiveness?  

Take notes after each visit, and revisit these questions as you sense God’s leading.

## A Simple Practice This Week  

> **Practice:** Attend a worship service at a church you’re considering, then spend 15 minutes afterward writing down what you observed about teaching, fellowship, and service.  

**Gentle next step:** Schedule a brief conversation (in person or via phone/email) with a pastor or elder to ask one of the reflection questions above that felt most pressing for you. Let them know you’re exploring and appreciate their honesty.

## Key Takeaways  

- Look for a church that reflects the biblical marks of teaching, fellowship, worship, and service (Acts 2:42‑42  
- Ask clear, grace‑filled questions about doctrine, community, mission, discipleship, and how the church handles doubt and Eph 4).  
- Use the six reflection questions to discern whether a congregation aligns with Scripture and your own spiritual needs.  
- Observe, take notes, and engage in conversation—don’t rush the decision.  
- Remember that joining a church is a commitment to mutual growth, not just personal preference.  
- Depend on prayer and the Holy Spirit’s leading throughout the process.
